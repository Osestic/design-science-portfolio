Gate Review 3 · Failure Modes and Effects Analysis

Failure modes, mitigations and residual risk

The FMEA was run on November 1 against the Gate Review 2 roller design. Its two highest risks, a ball that drifts on the cradle (RPN 392) and a wrong grade (RPN 288), drove the biggest GR3 changes: the rollers became a locating peg, and grading moved to a ratio against a same-model reference with a three-state result. Revised ratings below are projections from the design changes; none is claimed as verified until the POC tests in the validation plan are run.

Ratings use 1–10 scales for severity (S), occurrence (O) and detection (D, 10 = undetectable); RPN = S × O × D. The team's worksheet, with the root-cause write-up for the top item, is fmea.xlsx. Owners: Rhia (FMEA lead), with each row's owner shown.

Critical failure modes in detail

Ball not held on the cradle (RPN 392 → 56 projected)

  1. What fails and when. On the GR2 two-roller cradle the ball slid sideways each time the rollers turned, so the plate pressed a different spot from the one intended.
  2. Consequences. Users: a worn ball can pass, or repeat tests disagree, and trust in the product is lost. Non-users and environment: good balls thrown away. Product: its main function is degraded.
  3. Evidence for the ratings. S7: the heaviest needs (15% and 13%) are defeated. O8: drift was seen every time the alpha prototype's rollers were turned. D7: nothing sensed drift.
  4. Design change. The rollers and their motor are gone; a peg through one ball hole fixes the spot (Driver 3).
  5. Connected parameters. M4 × 0.7 × 8 mm steel set screw in the tapped center of P15, on the load-cell axis; ball holes are about 8 mm, so the peg locates without carrying load.
  6. POC representation. Identical peg, plate and load cell.
  7. Test. 5 balls × 20 re-seats; spread ≤ 5% of stiffness; single-press blind grading.
  8. Residual risk. The peg cannot find a soft spot elsewhere on the ball. The Instron data suggest used balls soften all over, but that rests on 10 balls.

Wrong grade shown (RPN 288 → 160 projected)

  1. What fails. A soft ball shows PLAY, a good ball shows RETIRE, or the player misreads the result.
  2. Consequences. Users keep a ball that costs points or discard good balls; waste and cost rise; disputes with other players.
  3. Evidence. S8: the core function. O6: thresholds are untested against bounce. D6: the user cannot see an error.
  4. Design change. Ratio to a same-model reference; frozen thresholds; a CHECK BALL state for invalid presses; icon + word + sound.
  5. Connected parameters. 2 mm press (about 87 N on a new ball), 80 samples/s, 0.5 N contact threshold.
  6. POC representation. Same force path and firmware logic; OLED text instead of the touchscreen.
  7. Test. Blind grading of ≥ 20 held-out balls against the USA Pickleball bounce test; ≥ 90% agreement.
  8. Residual risk. Remains the highest RPN after changes, which is why it is the first validation activity.

Wrong model or temperature (RPN 210 → 140 projected)

  1. What fails. A cold, hot or different-model ball is compared with the wrong reference.
  2. Consequences. Wrong grade; a cold new ball can look as soft as a warm worn one.
  3. Evidence. Pickleball Science found stiffness changes strongly between −1, 23 and 49 °C.
  4. Design change. Model selection with stored references; same-session reference ball; room-temperature instruction.
  5. Connected parameters. USA Pickleball's test condition is 70 ± 5 °F (about 18–24 °C).
  6. POC representation. POC tests record ball and room temperature with an external thermometer and run only inside that range.
  7. Test. Measure 3 new X-40s cold, at room temperature and warm; decide whether the MVP needs a sensor.
  8. Residual risk. Outdoor use in summer or winter; relies on the user following instructions.

Finger pinch at the press (safety; RPN 48 and 90 → 12 projected)

  1. What fails. A finger is under the Press Plate when it moves.
  2. Consequences. Injury to the user or a bystander child.
  3. Evidence. A stalled drive can reach 177 N after current limiting, above the 140 N quasi-static hand limit in ISO 10218-2, so force limiting alone does not protect a finger.
  4. Design change. The MVP door reed switch is mandatory: no test can start with the door open, and opening the door stops the motor without automatic restart. Start is enabled only after the door is shut on a ball.
  5. Connected parameters. 2 mm press at 2–4 mm/s; 69.05 mm minimum gap.
  6. POC representation. Not represented: the POC has no interlock, so it is run only by trained team members with the case closed and the power disconnect in reach.
  7. Test. Interlock trials with a non-human probe on the first MVP build.
  8. Residual risk. Interlock failure or bypass; addressed by the stall-current limit and the slow press speed.

  • Design drivers: RPN 392 reframed driver 3; RPN 288 is driver 1 (Technical Development Methods).
  • Parameter analysis: current limit, hard-stop gap and contact threshold (Safety and operating limits).
  • MVP: peg, reed switch, IR sensor, CHECK BALL state, steel rod (MVP).
