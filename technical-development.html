Gate Review 3 · Technical Development Methods

Five design drivers decide whether CuPickle works

Five focused questions decide whether CuPickle gives a trusted grade in 30 s or less. Drivers 2 and 4 are resolved by analysis; driver 1 has test evidence; driver 3 is decided (a locating peg and one press) and waits on the POC; driver 5 needs the player study.

The drivers come from the heaviest QFD needs (House of Quality), the hardest functions in the functional decomposition, the risks left open at downselection, and what the alpha prototype taught us: the ball drifted on the rollers and the frame was too large. Activity 20 framed driver 3 around two motorized rollers; the Instron test and the FMEA changed it to the peg question below. The original Activity 20 write-up is in the Appendix.

Driver 1: Playability grading (controlled experiment)

  1. Question. Does a plate press show a stiffness loss with play, and what ratio to a new ball marks a ball RETIRE?
  2. Why it matters. The grade is the product.
  3. Requirement and failure mode. Identifies low-performance balls (15%, the heaviest need); FMEA "wrong grade shown" (RPN 288).
  4. Why this method. Pickleball Science measured plate stiffness on Franklin X-40s falling 93.5 → 84.0 → 75.2 lb/in over two hours of play, the same property CuPickle measures, so a controlled compression test on our own balls is the most direct check.
  5. Application. Phase A pressed one new and one used ball to 1, 5 and 11 mm. Phase B pressed five new and five used balls at six marked positions each to 5 mm (60 runs).
  6. Inputs. Instron press with a flat plate and block (5 N display step), tared runs only, force interpolated at 1–5 mm. Used balls came from courts retiring them; play history unknown.
  7. Result. Used balls averaged 184.6 N and new 200.7 N at 5 mm (stiffness ratio 0.932). At 2 mm the gap is 11.5% (p = 0.045). Measured new-ball stiffness (about 37 N/mm) is 2.3× the published 16.4 N/mm, so only ratios are usable. Limits: 10 balls, unknown history, fixture differs from CuPickle.
  8. Decision. Grade on the ratio to a same-model new-ball reference, never on absolute stiffness. Press a fixed 2 mm: new balls reach about 87 N there, well inside the drive and load-cell limits, and the curve is linear.
  9. Validation. Repeat on X-40s with 0, 4 and 8 recorded games; confirm the ratio is within 0.05 of the published ratio; blind-grade ≥ 20 held-out balls against the bounce test (Validation, Activity 1).

Driver 2: Force-path accuracy (analytical model, then bench test)

  1. Question. Do frame flex, motor lag or nut backlash hide the wear signal?
  2. Why it matters. At 2 mm the worn-ball signal is about 10 N; any flex is read as ball deflection.
  3. Requirement. S2 (≤ 0.025 mm), S16 (%GRR < 10); FMEA "force reading wrong" (RPN 175).
  4. Why this method. A series compliance stack shows which part flexes most before anything is built, and is quick to update with CAD dimensions.
  5. Application. Summed the compliance of the load-cell bar, plates, carriage arm and screw and compared it with a new X-40.
  6. Inputs. 1/4 in. 6061-T6, spans from the drawings, 235 N worst-case stall thrust, 1.8° motor with 1/16 microstepping.
  7. Result. Machine compliance 0.00204 mm/N, 3.3% of the ball's; worn/new ratio error 0.005. Travel 0.0025 mm per microstep, lag ≤ 0.022 mm. Safety factors 1.33–19.7.
  8. Decision. Keep 1/4 in. plates; subtract steel-ball compliance; 0.5 N contact threshold so backlash is always taken up the same way; stall thrust limited to 177 N.
  9. Validation. Dial-indicator travel check; calibration with known masses; gauge R&R on the POC (Activity 2).

Driver 3: Ball location (selection comparison)

  1. Question. How is the ball held so every test presses a known spot?
  2. Why it matters. The alpha prototype's ball drifted on the rollers, and a random spot changes the reading.
  3. Requirement. S1, S16; FMEA "ball not held" (RPN 392, the highest).
  4. Why this method. Three candidates could be compared on drift, coverage, time and parts using data we already had.

  1. Application and inputs. Phase B position data and the cycle-time budget.
  2. Result. Weakest positions were only 4–9% below the other five on the same ball, so used balls were softer all over, not at one spot. One random spot graded about 75% correctly in-sample, four spots 91%, six 100% (Figure V4).
  3. Decision. An M4 × 0.7 × 8 mm set-screw peg (P14) at the center of the Load Cell Plate; one press per ball. Testing several spots and soft-spot detection are deferred beyond the MVP.
  4. Limitation. 75% comes from random spots on 10 balls with an in-sample cut-off; a fixed spot removes position scatter but has not been tested.
  5. Validation. Single-press blind grading of ≥ 20 balls; 5 balls × 20 re-seats, spread ≤ 5% of stiffness.

Driver 4: Test cycle time (analytical model)

  1. Question. Does one press fit in 30 s, and is the amplifier fast enough?
  2. Why it matters. Players judge a ball in about 3 s today.
  3. Requirement. S7, ≤ 30 s.
  4. Why this method. A time budget and a samples-per-millimetre model show the margin before hardware exists.
  5. Inputs. 5 mm approach and 8 mm retract at 10 mm/s, 1 mm contact search, 2 mm press, 6 s to load and close (assumed), 2 s result.
  6. Result. About 10.8 s at 2 mm/s and 10.1 s at 4 mm/s. At 10 samples/s the HX711 gives too few points; at 80 samples/s the overshoot is under 1.5 N.
  7. Uncertainty. The 6 s loading time dominates and is assumed.
  8. Decision. HX711 RATE pin high (80 samples/s); press at 2 mm/s, or 4 mm/s if stiffness changes by less than 1%.
  9. Validation. Firmware timestamps on the POC; timed first-use trials in the player study.

Driver 5: Player trust (customer and usability study)

  1. Question. Which result format makes a player retire a ball on the result alone?
  2. Why it matters. Players buy confidence, not a number.
  3. Requirement. S5 (≥ 2-point gain), S8 (≤ 2 actions).
  4. Why this method. Trust can only be measured with players.
  5. Application. ≥ 21 weekly competitive adult players, including older players, check coded balls by hand and with CuPickle and rate their confidence.
  6. Inputs. Result as large check or X, the word PLAY or RETIRE, and a distinct sound (Activity 26); one start control at ≤ 22.2 N (ADA 309.4).
  7. Result. None yet.
  8. Decision so far. The icon + word + sound format replaces the GR2 text-only readout.
  9. Validation. Validation, Activity 3.
