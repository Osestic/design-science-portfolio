Design gap and market opportunity

What existing products do well. Instrumented testers give precise, repeatable, objective results, and the Go/No-Go shows a pass/fail answer can be delivered in under a minute by a device light enough for an event. Crack-resistant balls reduce how often failures happen.

Where they fall short. Every instrumented tester is built for manufacturers, labs or paddle conformance. None gives a player or club an in-service ball-condition reading at the court, and the only ball-specific design is an unreleased patent. The manual check is fast but subjective: 52.8% of players rate their confidence in it at 3 or lower out of 7, and balls soften well before damage becomes visible.

The design gap: knowing a ball's playing condition before visible damage appears, with an result players can trust, in about the time of a manual check. This matters because consistency is the top purchase criterion (52.8%), players lack confidence (52.8% rate 1–3), and 55.6% find an objective test useful, rising to 64.7% of frequent players [4].

  • What makes the direction distinct: no commercial ball-condition tester exists at any price.
  • Targets that drive viability: time per test and user confidence, followed by price.
  • Risks to differentiation: ADC already makes both a ball-capable press and a portable pass/fail device, and could combine them; the airflow patent application may constrain some methods; crack-resistant balls, including a patented graphene ball [14], may shrink the problem; and the facility owner we interviewed saw no need (P4) [4].
  • Barriers: the Equipment Standards Manual's approval tests [2] should be replicated or complemented, never presented as certification.
  • Evidence the gap is worth pursuing: survey interest rises with play frequency, and interviewees see value for competitive and club settings, though no professionals have been interviewed yet.

Product positioning

Objectivity scale (team assessment): 1 = judgment by feel; 3 = instrumented pass/fail against a fixed threshold; 5 = repeatable numeric measurement of a physical property. Why these axes: survey respondents lack confidence in their own judgment (52.8% rate 1–3) and want an objective test (55.6%), which gives the vertical axis. Other players on court rank fast analysis second of eleven QFD requirements, and the manual check takes about 3 s, which gives the horizontal axis [4][5]. Price is kept off the axes and shown in the point labels instead.
