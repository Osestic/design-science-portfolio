Section 03

Product Definition

What the team intends to develop and how success will be measured, without committing to how it works.

QFD Survey n=36 Interviews n=4 Benchmarking & standards

The product

Our goal is to develop a pickleball condition tester that helps players and clubs determine whether a ball is still in good playing condition before or during play. It gives players a practical, objective check instead of relying on visual inspection or personal intuition. Surveys and interviews show that players care most about performance consistency, confidence in their equipment, durability and price. Players already have their own ways of checking balls, but those methods are subjective and inconsistent. The opportunity is a method that tests a pickleball without taking much time or being difficult to use.

The main user is the individual frequent player; clubs, tournament organizers and other facilities are the main purchasers and stakeholders (see Customers). Clubs and organizers need testing to be quick so it does not slow games, and players do not want to wait for someone else to finish testing. The product will be considered successful if it helps players feel more confident about their balls, reduces ball-related problems during games, and does both without damaging the ball or taking too much time.

What the QFD concludes

The QFD relates eleven customer requirements to thirteen specifications, weighted separately for four stakeholders [3]. Its main conclusions are:

  • The player's top three requirements are improves gameplay, increases confidence in quality and doesn't damage equipment. They map to the two outcome specifications (decrease in games with malfunction; user confidence) and the no-damage specification.
  • Doesn't damage equipment is the only requirement in every stakeholder's top four, so it is treated as a constraint.
  • Stakeholders disagree on price: it is first for the manufacturer and second for court owners, but last for other players on court.
  • A portability cluster (weight, footprint, capacity) and a confidence cluster (confidence, tracking, durability) are each internally coupled, and the design tension runs between them.

Requirements, objectives and constraints

Requirements

  • Does not damage the ball being tested
  • Results are repeatable
  • No special training needed
  • Testing does not significantly interrupt a game
  • Works with commonly used pickleballs

Objectives

  • Increase player confidence in their balls
  • Reduce games affected by ball failure
  • Give more useful information than a visual or manual check
  • Keep the product affordable
  • Make it portable enough to take to the court

Constraints

  • Affordable for the consumer and club market
  • Small and light enough to carry
  • Testing cannot take too long
  • Testing cannot permanently damage the ball
  • No laboratory or specialized facility required
  • No claim of USA Pickleball certification [5]

Main tradeoffs

Cost and size versus testing capability. Industrial testers perform more detailed measurements, but they are far larger and more expensive than makes sense for a player or a small club. Our product will likely give up some measurement detail to stay portable and affordable.

Accuracy versus testing time. More measurements give better information about a ball's condition, but take longer. Since other players, court owners and tournament organizers are affected by testing time, the product must give useful results without reducing playing time.

Characteristics most important to success

  1. Reduction in games affected by ball failure and
  2. Player confidence in ball quality: the main measures of whether the product solves the problem.
  3. Testing time: players must be able to test quickly and return to their game.
  4. Product cost and
  5. Product weight: these separate the product from the large industrial testers.
  6. Testing without damaging the ball: necessary for the product to be useful at all.

Each characteristic, with its metric, target, evidence, relationships and tradeoffs, is on the Characteristics & Targets page.
