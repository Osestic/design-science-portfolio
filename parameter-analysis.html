3. Press depth and measurement mode

Question. Should CuPickle press to a force cap or to a fixed depth, and how far? Candidates: 89 N force cap; 133 N cap; fixed 2, 3.5 or 5 mm depth.

Evidence. Force is linear with compression to 5 mm (R² ≥ 0.997), so the slope over any window is a valid stiffness. Used balls were 11.5% softer at 2 mm (p = 0.045) and 8.0% at 5 mm (p = 0.020) (Validation). New balls reached about 87 N at 2 mm and 200 N at 5 mm.

Decision. A fixed 2 mm press from a 0.5 N contact. It keeps the force near 87 N on a new ball, inside the drive margin and the load-cell range; it presses less than a third of the 6.35 mm ASTM F1888 certification depth, which protects the ball; and it takes half the time of a 5 mm press. Tradeoff: at 2 mm the new–used gap has a larger p-value than at 5 mm, so the POC records full curves to 5 mm during validation and the depth will be revisited if single-press agreement falls short.

4. Drive: motor and lead screw

Inputs. Iverntech 42HD4027-42 NEMA 17 with integrated Tr8×8 screw: 0.40 N·m holding torque, 8 mm lead (2 mm pitch, 4 starts), brass nut. Mean thread diameter dm ≈ 7.0 mm, flank half-angle α = 15°, brass-on-steel friction μ = 0.10–0.25 (lubricated to dry).

Equations. Lead angle λ = tan−1(L / πdm) = 20.0°. Efficiency η = (cos α − μ tan λ) / (cos α + μ cot λ). Thrust F = 2πηT / L; torque needed T = F·L / (2πη).

Table 2. Power-screw model. "Worst-case ball" scales the stiffest legal ball (30.1 N/mm) by the 2.3× factor between our Instron readings and published values, giving about 140 N at 2 mm.

Result and decision. A new ball needs 0.15–0.21 N·m, a 1.4–2.0× margin under the 0.30 N·m current setting. The 8 mm lead also limits thrust: a Tr8×2 screw would give 199–458 N, enough to exceed the load cell's 294 N ultimate rating and four times slower. The screw is not self-locking (back-drives when μ < tan λ cos α = 0.35); with power off the ball pushes the carriage away, the safe direction. Sensitivity. Thrust varies ±25% with friction. The very stiffest legal ball with a dry nut would need 0.34 N·m, above the current setting; the stall-thrust bench test confirms the real margin, and the nut is greased at assembly.

5. Driver current and overload protection

The 20 kg load cell (196 N rated) has a 120% safe overload (235 N) and 150% ultimate (294 N). At full current and μ = 0.10 the stall thrust is 235 N, a safety factor of only 1.0. Decision: set the TMC2209 run current so stall torque is ≤ 0.30 N·m, giving ≤ 177 N (SF 1.33 to safe overload, 1.66 to ultimate). Layers of protection: firmware stops at the 2 mm depth or at 150 N, whichever comes first; the driver current limit; the P07 flange hard stop; and step-loss detection. Check: measure stall force once with the digital force gauge (validation activity 2).

8. Displacement resolution

Travel per full step = 8 mm / 200 = 0.040 mm, which fails S2 (0.025 mm). With 1/16 microstepping it is 0.0025 mm. Under load the rotor lags by θ ≈ (2/π)·1.8°·sin−1(T/Thold): 0.010 mm at 0.15 N·m and 0.022 mm at 0.30 N·m. The lag grows smoothly with force, so the steel-ball calibration removes it. Decision: 1/16 microstepping; position from step count, checked with a 0.01 mm dial indicator.

Effect. Uncorrected, a new ball reads 3.2% less stiff than it is (15.85 vs. 16.37 N/mm), but the worn/new ratio reads 0.809 against a true 0.804: an error of 0.005, a tenth of the 0.05 tolerance. Our balls measured stiffer than published (37.3 N/mm), which raises the frame's share to 7.6%; even then a true ratio of 0.932 reads 0.937, still an error of only 0.005. Decision: accept the frame and subtract the steel-ball compliance at calibration.

10. Stroke, clearance and hard stop

The flange on the Anti-Rotation Rod (P07, Ø.630 in.) stops the Carriage at a 69.05 mm gap between the Press Plate and the Load Cell Plate. Largest legal ball 75.4 mm → at most 6.35 mm compression, exactly the ASTM F1888 certification depth; smallest legal ball 72.9 mm → at most 3.85 mm. A 2 mm press brings the gap to 70.9–73.4 mm, so the stop is never reached in normal use. Retracted gap must clear the largest ball plus 5 mm for loading (A): ≥ 80.4 mm. From there a press ends 7.0 mm (largest ball) to 9.5 mm (smallest ball) below home, and the stop sits 11.35 mm below home. Machining the stop into the rod removes the separate collar and the step of setting it.

13. Temperature

Pickleball Science [3] found stiffness changes strongly between −1, 23 and 49 °C, so a cold new ball could grade like a warm worn ball. MVP decision: no temperature sensor in the product; grading uses a same-model reference and the quick-start card asks for room-temperature balls. POC testing: ball and room temperature are read with an external thermometer and tests run only at 70 ± 5 °F, the USA Pickleball test condition. Three new X-40s will be measured cold, at room temperature and warm to decide whether the MVP needs a sensor.

14. Safety and operating limits

ISO 10218-2 (Annex M, formerly ISO/TS 15066) gives 140 N as the maximum quasi-static force for hands and fingers. The 2 mm press (about 87 N) is below it, but a stalled drive can reach 177 N, so force limiting alone does not protect a trapped finger.

15. User interface and ergonomics

Controls follow ADA 309.4: one hand, no tight grasping or twisting, ≤ 5 lbf (22.2 N) to operate. Decisions: a large on-screen Start target and an on/off rocker; result as a check or X, the word PLAY or RETIRE and a distinct sound, kept on screen until the door is opened; door opening ≥ 115 mm (ball plus fingers). Aesthetic direction (Act 17): dark upper shell, light base and lime accent carried over from GR2. Approval is rated on a 1–7 scale in the player study, against the QFD target of ≥ 75% of players rating it 5 or higher (Validation).

16. Power and battery sizing

Coil dissipation at full current ≈ 2 × I²R = 2 × 1.7² × 1.7 Ω ≈ 9.8 W (A). At 70% current, 4.8 W plus 1.5 W for electronics is about 6.3 W during a 10 s test: ≈ 0.018 Wh per test, with the motor de-energized between tests. A 9 V NiMH 175 mAh cell (1.6 Wh, 80% usable) gives about 70 tests and must supply about 0.7 A, near its limit. Decision: the MVP uses the Energizer NH22NBP 9 V NiMH cell: clip-in and replaceable, NiMH avoids the lithium fire and shipping requirements, and it is charged on the main board over USB-C. The TMC2209 runs from 4.75 V, so it works from 9 V. Current per test is measured on the POC to confirm that one charge covers a playing session; if it does not, a higher-capacity NiMH pack of the same chemistry is the fallback.
