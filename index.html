ME 455 · Gate Review 1 · Team 1

Nobody should lose a point to a cracked ball.

A pickleball goes soft, warps or cracks long before most players can see it. The game stops, the point is replayed, and the equipment gets thrown out on a guess. This portfolio documents the customer, market and competitive evidence behind a product that tells a player whether their ball or paddle is still fit for play — before the match, not during it.

Project and product name

Working title: PaddleCheck. A tentative name for a pickleball equipment condition tester. The team has not yet committed to a product name.

Sector and use context

Recreational and competitive sports equipment, specifically accessories for pickleball. The product is used courtside — in a gym bag, on a bench between games, or at the equipment rack of a club or tournament — not in a lab.

The observed nuisance

Pickleballs and paddles degrade continuously and fail abruptly. A ball softens, warps or cracks; a paddle develops a dead spot or delaminates. The failure is usually discovered mid-point, when the ball bounces in a way nobody expected. The game pauses, equipment is swapped, and in a competitive match the point may be replayed. Players currently judge condition by bouncing the ball, squeezing it, tapping the paddle, or simply looking — methods they perform routinely but do not fully trust.

Target customer and user

The primary user is an active recreational or competitive player who plays often enough to wear equipment out. Interviews pointed to a second, possibly stronger customer: the club, facility or tournament organizer who buys and manages equipment in volume and carries the cost of replacing it.

Why it matters

Competition is the top-ranked reason our respondents play (48.5% ranked it first). Equipment that behaves unpredictably attacks the part of the sport players care about most: a fair, uninterrupted, competitive game. The cost is not only money spent on replacements but points lost, matches interrupted, and confidence undermined.

Supporting evidence, in brief

A 40-response survey (36 on the revised instrument) and two long-form interviews with regular players, supported by a customer journey map and competitive benchmarking of the three equipment testers with published specifications. Damage is near-universal, detection confidence is split almost evenly, and interest in an objective test is highest among the most frequent players — 64.7% of those who play regularly or often, against 47.4% of occasional players.

Proposed product direction

A portable, consumer- and club-priced device that gives an objective read on the condition of a pickleball or paddle in well under a minute, without damaging the equipment it tests. The team has deliberately not fixed the sensing method; the QFD targets describe what the product must achieve, not how.

Market value proposition

Every instrument that measures pickleball equipment condition today is industrial: the three benchmarked testers sell for $2,900 to $7,200 and weigh 22.8 to 41 kg. Nothing exists for the player or the club, and no ball tester exists at any price. The opportunity is to bring an objective, trustworthy condition check to the courtside at roughly $100 and 7.5 kg.

Product goal

The goal of this project is to design a product that will let pickleball players and clubs objectively determine whether a ball or paddle is still fit for play, before it fails during a game.

What is PaddleCheck?

In one line

A portable tester that tells a player or a club whether a pickleball or paddle is still fit for play — at the court, in under a minute, without harming the equipment it tests.

What it is not

Not a more durable ball, not an officiating or conformance tool, and not a coaching device. The project scope is assessing the equipment players already own.

Who it is for

Frequent players — the 7.48 million Americans who play eight or more times a year — and the clubs, facilities and tournament organisers who buy equipment for them.

Why now

Pickleball participation grew 171.8% in three years. The equipment base is growing far faster than any means of judging its condition, which still does not exist for balls at any price.
