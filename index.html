ME 455 · Gate Review 1 · Team 1

Nobody should lose a point to a cracked ball.

A pickleball goes soft, warps or cracks long before most players can see it. The rally is lost or replayed, and the ball gets thrown out on a guess. This portfolio documents the customer, market and competitive evidence behind a product that tells players and clubs whether a ball still plays like a good ball — before the match, not during it.

Project and product name

Project: pickleball ball-condition screening. Product name: [TEAM: tentative product name; the earlier working title “PaddleCheck” no longer fits a ball-only product]

Sector and use context

Sports equipment accessories for pickleball. The product is used where balls are chosen and retired: on the court before and after play, and at a club's ball caddy or a tournament desk, often with no staff present.

The observed nuisance

Pickleballs degrade continuously and fail abruptly. A ball softens, warps or cracks, and the failure is usually discovered mid-rally, when the ball bounces in a way nobody expected. Under the rules a soft ball earns no replay and is swapped only if every player agrees. Players judge condition today by looking, bouncing, squeezing or hitting the ball, a check that takes about three seconds but that 52.8% of survey respondents do not trust.

Target customer and user

The user is the frequent, competitive player who plays at least weekly. The likely purchaser is the club, facility or tournament director who supplies and retires balls: all three player interviewees would rather use a tester at their club than buy one. One facility owner, however, saw no need, so the purchaser is still a hypothesis.

Why it matters

Competition is the top reason our respondents play (48.5% rank it first), and consistent performance is their top equipment criterion (52.8%). An unpredictable ball attacks exactly what these players value: a fair, uninterrupted game.

Supporting evidence, in brief

A 40-response survey (36 on the revised form), four interviews (three players and one owner of two indoor venues), a customer journey map, a QFD, competitive benchmarking, a patent and standards search, and national market data. Damage is common (72.2% have played with a cracked ball), confidence is low, and interest in an objective test is highest among frequent players (64.7% against 47.4% of occasional players). The U.S. has 24.3 million players, 7.5 million of them CORE players who play eight or more times a year.

Proposed product direction

A ball-condition screening product that gives a quick keep-or-retire answer tied to how the ball plays (its bounce against the USA Pickleball standard), works courtside or unattended without special skill, does not damage the ball, and does not claim official certification. The team has deliberately not chosen a sensing method.

Market value proposition

Every instrument that measures pickleball equipment today is built for manufacturers, labs or paddle conformance: benchmarked testers cost $2,900 to $5,000 to buy, and the Go/No-Go rents for $299 a month. No commercial tester checks a ball in service. The opportunity is an objective check in 30 seconds or less, close to the bounce test players already do, so that clubs keep only good balls in play and players stop losing points to balls they could not see were bad.

Product goal

The goal of this project is to design a product that will let clubs, tournament directors and frequent competitive players quickly and objectively determine whether a pickleball still plays like a good ball, before it fails during a game.
