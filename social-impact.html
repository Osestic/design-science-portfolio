Gate Review 2 · Concept & Prototyping

Social Impact Statement

Gate Review 1 described the context the product would enter: a social, mostly outdoor sport played by people from childhood to their eighties, whose balls end up in landfill (Contextual Considerations). Now that the concept is DinkCheck, an enclosed motorized compression station, the question becomes more specific: what do this mechanism, these materials and this verdict do to the people and places around it?

Decisions already made because of this analysis

Who benefits, and who bears the cost

The benefits fall mostly on frequent, competitive players who can afford a dedicated device; casual players, who in our interviews preferred a tester provided by their club [2], gain little unless they borrow one. A product priced well above the $100 target would widen that gap. The safety risks fall on the owner and on anyone nearby, including children at public courts, which is why the guards are mechanical as well as in software. Environmental costs fall on communities near landfills and on workers in supply chains the team has not yet examined, people who receive none of the product's benefits.

Tradeoffs

  • Customer value against sustainability. A stricter threshold catches more soft balls, giving more consistent play, but sends more balls to landfill. The team chose to set the threshold by playability, so a ball that still plays well is kept.
  • Technical performance against cost. Two motors and a load cell give the most repeatable reading of the five concepts, and also the highest cost (Downselection).
  • Safety against convenience. Enclosing the squeeze behind a door removes the pinch point from reach but adds a step to loading; the window keeps the test visible.
  • Portability against safety and cost. A battery would free the unit from outlets but adds a battery hazard, weight, cost and another component to dispose of.

Conclusion

Most consequential positive impact

Consistent, fair games: players can retire balls that have gone soft on evidence instead of by argument, and keep balls that still play well.

Most significant potential harm

A verdict that looks authoritative before its thresholds are validated. A wrong “Non-playable” wastes balls and money; a wrong “Playable” undermines the trust the product exists to create.

Current mitigation

Grade relative to a new ball, in plain words, with no claim of approval; set thresholds by playability; cap force and travel mechanically and in software.

Questions for detailed design

Does DinkCheck's verdict agree with the USA Pickleball bounce test and with players' judgment? Does it reduce or increase ball waste in practice? Can older players read and operate it? Can it reach $100 without unsafe batteries or hidden supply-chain costs?
